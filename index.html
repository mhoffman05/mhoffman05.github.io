DCS 105 · Calling Bull in a Digital World · Fall 2025

Running on Bad Data

A website debunking sports science claims made to endurance athletes. It examines three products: Nomio, a broccoli-sprout supplement marketed with weak studies and an unlabeled graph; sodium bicarbonate from Maurten and Bicarb Shop, whose race calculators predict gains well beyond what controlled studies show; and Garmin and Coros watches, whose VO₂ max and race predictions are algorithm estimates presented as measurements. The site uses visual analysis, analysis in R, and public-facing explanation to refute each claim.

What I learned: how to read a graph for what it leaves out, and how to check a marketing claim against the actual literature.

DCS 236 · VECINA: Civic Data Design With and For Community · Winter 2026

VECINA

VECINA is a community resource platform run by the Woonasquatucket River Watershed Council for Providence, RI neighborhoods. On the class healthcare team, I helped draft a new healthcare section of the site: compiling and cleaning resource data in Google Sheets (addresses, contact information, English and Spanish descriptions, and map coordinates) and curating reliable sources for Vecinita, the project's AI assistant.

What I learned: real-world data is messy, and when you design with a community partner, their needs set the priorities.

DCS 340 · Evolutionary Computing · Fall 2026

Classical Optimization Module

Notebooks from the Introduction to Classical Methods in Optimization module, where I explored how to find minima and maxima, first in one dimension and then in higher-dimensional spaces, before moving on to evolutionary algorithms. I worked through them in Google Colab with Gemini and connected them to GitHub.

What I learned: optimization is the same question I ask in physiology and training (what's the best outcome, and what limits it?) written in math and code.

Experience

Bavis Lab, Bates College

Summer 2026

Summer research student, respiratory physiology

Studied breathing in neonatal rats across two projects. We found that the drop in ventilation after returning from hypoxia to normal oxygen was not matched by a drop in metabolic rate. I also worked on a rat model of SIDS combining inflammation (LPS) with sustained hypoxia. Methods included head-out plethysmography, injections, simple surgery, LabChart, and GraphPad Prism.
