About

I'm a Biology major and Digital & Computational Studies minor at Bates College, a physiology researcher, and a distance runner on the Bates cross country and track & field teams.

I learn best by doing. The lab bench, the track, and a blank notebook in Colab have all taught me the same thing: an idea only counts once you've tested it. That's what pulls me toward physiology, and toward computing as a way to check claims instead of taking them on faith.

Running is where my interest in sports science started. Endurance athletes are surrounded by products and metrics that promise to make us faster, and a lot of that "science" falls apart under a closer look. My DCS work gave me tools to take those claims apart. My biology coursework and research taught me what the body is really doing. I'm hoping to go to graduate school and work in biomedical research.

DCS 105 · Calling Bull in a Digital World · Fall 2025

Running on Bad Data

A website debunking endurance-based sports science product claims. I used visual BS-busting, BS-busting with R, and public BS-busting to take apart three examples: Nomio (weak studies and an unlabeled graph), sodium bicarbonate from Maurten and Bicarb Shop (race-time predictions far beyond what controlled studies show), and Garmin & Coros watches (VO₂ max and race predictions treated as measurements when they're algorithm estimates).

What I learned: how to read a graph for what it leaves out, and how to test a marketing claim against the actual literature.

DCS 236 · VECINA: Civic Data Design With and For Community · Winter 2026

VECINA

VECINA is a Woonasquatucket River Watershed Council platform that helps Providence, RI residents find resources and advocate for their neighborhoods. Our class drafted a new section of the website and cleaned the project's existing data in Google Sheets so the community could use it.

Research & Experience

Bavis Lab, Bates College

Summer 2026

Summer Research Student · Animal (respiratory) physiology · Dr. Ryan Bavis

Worked on two projects on breathing in neonatal rats. In the first, we measured interscapular temperature, ventilation, and CO₂ production at the same time. We found that the ventilatory depression after switching from hypoxia (12% O₂) back to normoxia was not mirrored by a drop in metabolic rate. The second tested a rat model of SIDS that combines an inflammatory stressor (LPS) with sustained hypoxia during a critical developmental window. The sample was too small for statistical conclusions, but the early data suggest sustained hypoxia changed breathing patterns while prior LPS exposure added no further change.

Head-out plethysmography · intraperitoneal injections · simple surgical procedures · LabChart · GraphPad Prism · reviewed 17 primary papers
