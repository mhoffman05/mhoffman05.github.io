The formula

Three ingredients. No fillers.

I'm a Biology major at Bates College with a minor in Digital & Computational Studies. What pulls it all together is sports science — especially endurance performance, where marketing often moves faster than the research.

My DCS coursework taught me to be the person in the room asking "what's the evidence?" In DCS 105 I built a site debunking popular endurance-sport claims; in DCS 236 I cleaned real community data and drafted new content for the VECINA platform. Now in DCS 340 I'm learning to let algorithms evolve solutions to hard problems.

This portfolio is my lab notebook: the studies I've run, the tools I trust, and the workflow that ties them together.

05.

FAQ

Questions I get about what I do.

Why combine biology and computing?

Biology tells me what questions matter for performance. Computing lets me test them at scale — cleaning data, running models, and checking whether an effect is real or just noise.

What does "sports science" mean here?

Mostly endurance physiology: training, recovery, fueling, and the supplements and ergogenic aids marketed to athletes — and the evidence (or lack of it) behind them.

How do you use AI tools like Claude and Gemini?

As assistants, not authors. I use Claude to plan and debug in VS Code, and Gemini inside Colab to iterate on notebooks. I review everything and version it on GitHub, so the history of my thinking is visible.

Where can I see your code?

On GitHub. The optimization notebooks are linked in the Studies section above.
